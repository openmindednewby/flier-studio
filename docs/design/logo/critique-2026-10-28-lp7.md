## A "Dealt F"

- **1 Distinctiveness 2.** In `r1-lineup32.png` the Flipboard stand-in (4th) and A (5th) are the same grammar: an F
  built from separated rectangular blocks, stem plus two arms, dark on light. The few degrees of arm rotation that
  make it "dealt" vanish at 32 px; what is left is a block F. Cover the name and this is Flipboard's F, or any
  geometric-sans F monogram. Yes, it resembles Flipboard's F, at exactly the size a favicon is seen.
- 2 Visibility 4: ink 0.348 at 16 px light, 0.346 dark, stem and arms separate.
- 3 Useability 4: one colour 0.275, reversed 0.278, 0 px outside the maskable circle.
- 4 Memorability 2: a cold reader says "an F"; the dealing idea is not recalled.
- 5 Universality 4: no trend tell, no gradient.
- 6 Timelessness 3: three parts, simple; but simple-and-generic, the "too basic" complaint again.
- 7 Craft 3: in `r1-header360.png` the tilted arms read as a misaligned glyph rather than an intent.
- 8 Appropriate 3: square paper family respected; the print-shop raw material is not visible.
- 9 Motion 4: stem then arms dealt in, token timings, ends on the still, reduced-motion still present.

Top fixes:
1. Drop the stem-plus-two-blocks construction outright: it is Flipboard's grammar and no rotation rescues it at 32 px.
2. If a letterform stays, draw the F as ONE sheet whose top arm is a corner folded back (the fold showing the back of
   the paper as a 2-unit gap), so the silhouette is a sheet with a fold, not three blocks.
3. Re-run S6 with the Flipboard stand-in beside it at 32 px; the two silhouettes must differ in outline, not in angle.

## B "Fan deck"

- **1 Distinctiveness 2.** A four-strip fan on a rivet is the stock swatch-book icon (Lucide `swatch-book`, Font
  Awesome `swatchbook`, every Pantone-guide pictogram). `r2-stock24.png` places it next to the stock palette glyph and
  the family resemblance is plain. It reads "colour picker", not "fliers".
- 2 Visibility 4: ink 0.276 at 16 px, strips stay separate.
- 3 Useability 4: one colour 0.269, reversed 0.272, safe circle clean.
- 4 Memorability 3: easy to describe ("a paint-chip fan"), but recalled as the generic object, not this brand.
- 5 Universality 3: the object is tied to the Pantone / paint-shop market.
- 6 Timelessness 4: one idea, five parts, durable.
- 7 Craft 4: even strip gaps, rivet hole clean, mask cut-outs true.
- 8 Appropriate 2: the swatch fan is the theme picker, a secondary feature; the product's job (a set of fliers from one
  form) is not in the mark.
- 9 Motion 5: the fan opens about its own rivet, staggered by `fast`, ends on the still. Best motion of the three.

Top fixes:
1. Retire the fan as the mark: the silhouette is a stock icon and no detail change moves it out of that family.
2. Keep its motion idea (parts opening about one pivot) for whichever mark replaces it: it is the strongest motion here.
3. If kept as a secondary in-app glyph (theme picker), say so on the page; it cannot be the brand mark.

## C "In flight"

- 1 Distinctiveness 3: no competitor in `r3-lineup32.png` shares the outline. But in `r3-header1280.png` the
  sheet-plus-detached-chip reads as an italic "J." or a pen nib before it reads as paper.
- 2 Visibility 3: ink 0.342 at 16 px, but in `r3-16-full-light.png` the curl is lost and the sheet becomes a dark
  tilted tag.
- 3 Useability 4: one colour 0.292, reversed 0.295, inside the safe circle (`r3-icon512-safe.png`).
- 4 Memorability 3: "a leaning sheet with a bit off the corner".
- 5 Universality 4: no trend tell.
- 6 Timelessness 3: two parts, one idea; the idea holds.
- **7 Craft 2.** The "curl" is a separate wedge 3 units below the cut corner (`construction-r3.png`): physically a curl
  stays attached and shows the back of the sheet; here it reads as a corner that broke off. The four-sided sheet with
  one curved long edge looks like a shard, not paper. The construction sheet's own notes are clipped at the right edge.
- 8 Appropriate 3: "a flier in flight" is the right idea from the name; the drawing loses "made of paper".
- **9 Motion 2.** `r3-intro.png`: the sheet only fades in place and the chip fades in after it. Nothing flies and
  nothing curls; the intro does not act out the mark's own story.

Top fixes:
1. Draw the curl as an attached lifted corner: the fold line continuous with the sheet, the turned-back corner shown as
   the paper's back (a 2-unit gap or second value), no free-floating wedge; at 16 px the corner at least 30% of the
   sheet's height.
2. Straighten the sheet to true paper proportion (1:1.414) with straight edges, tilted as a whole; let the lift and the
   curl carry the flight, not a skewed outline.
3. Rebuild the intro: the sheet travels about 10 px up and right over `slow` while the corner rolls open from the fold
   line (scale from the fold, not opacity), ending on the still.
